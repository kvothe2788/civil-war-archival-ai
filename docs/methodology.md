# Method and evidence standards

## Scope

This case study combines documentary research, oral history and exploratory geospatial analysis in the Matarrosa del Sil area. It is an ongoing investigation, not an archaeological excavation or a forensic identification.

## Evidence handling

Each meaningful claim should carry its source, date, relevant page or image when available, and a statement of what the source actually shows. Distinguish:

1. **Observed:** content directly checked in a source.
2. **Reported:** a claim attributed to a witness or secondary account.
3. **Inferred:** an interpretation requiring assumptions.
4. **Unknown:** information that the available evidence does not establish.

Maintain corrections and negative findings. A modern map label does not establish the name or infrastructure at a past date. A search with no hits does not prove a record does not exist.

## AI assistance and human review

Language models can help locate leads, organize notes, suggest searches and assist with transcription. They can also fabricate references, conflate similarly named people, misunderstand historical context or overstate what a source supports. Source existence, quotation, transcription, dates, relationships and important inferences need verification against the underlying document.

## Geospatial reasoning

Historic aerial photographs and maps postdate or precede the event by different amounts. Present-day vectors and interpolated elevation models do not reconstruct the terrain at a historical date. Road works, mine spoil, vegetation, resolution and georeferencing errors can all affect comparisons.

Sampling elevation along a mapped watercourse is a screening method. The line may not follow the actual channel; elevation differences over a window are not measurements of a waterfall. Field and archival verification remain necessary.
